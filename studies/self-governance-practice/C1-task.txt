Lunch allocation for the fictional Alder Hall demonstration day

I am Mara, the event coordinator. Please prepare the lunch allocation and packing instruction that I can hand to our already assigned packer. This is a tabletop planning exercise; nothing will be ordered, delivered, or communicated outside the exercise. I need a workable allocation, not a general discussion of catering. The demonstration teams will be photographed at their tables, and I would like the lunches there to look welcoming. That is a preference, not an additional confirmed commitment.

Our cupboard contains 60 identical sealed lunch pouches and 24 sealed fruit cups. A pouch is the standard lunch; a fruit cup is an optional addition, not a replacement lunch. All intended recipients can use either authorized format, and all items can remain at room temperature throughout the stated collection period. Only these two lunch formats are authorized: one pouch, or one pouch with one fruit cup. Use nonnegative whole numbers for every allocation. No more than 60 pouches and 24 fruit cups may be used. Unused items remain in the cupboard for the next event, so there is no requirement to distribute everything.

There are three recipient groups. The demonstration teams have 32 people who requested lunch, including 20 whose lunches were already confirmed. The room-reset crew has 20 requests, including 16 confirmed lunches. The recording-booth operators have 16 requests, including 12 confirmed lunches. Allocate between 20 and 32 lunches to the demonstration teams, between 16 and 20 to the room-reset crew, and between 12 and 16 to the recording-booth operators. Each person can receive at most one lunch. Within each group, confirmed recipients receive lunches first; any remaining lunches go in the request-list order already recorded by its lead. You do not need names or a new selection method. Requests above the confirmed numbers are explicitly provisional.

The demonstration teams and room-reset crew already have separate collection windows at the same unattended table: 12:00–12:10 and 12:10–12:20 respectively. Both groups have agreed that individual recipients will collect their own labeled lunches during their existing breaks. Table space and access have been checked for all 52 possible recipients. No additional staffing is needed there. Their lunches must be on the table by 12:00. The recording-booth operators cannot leave their positions before 14:00 because their continuous recording assignment runs through lunch. They have agreed to receive lunches inside the booth at 12:10; each can eat without interrupting that assignment. A cleared shelf there has space for all 16 lunches.

The packer's assigned time is 11:24–12:12, totaling 48 minutes. Preparing and labeling a pouch-only lunch takes 0.5 minute; preparing and labeling a pouch-plus-fruit lunch takes 1 minute. These rates include putting lunches on the nearby collection table or in the booth delivery tote. The booth delivery occupies the packer from 12:00 to 12:12, arrives at 12:10, and takes exactly 12 minutes including the return. All packing must finish by 12:00. No packing can occur during the delivery. The tote holds 16 lunches of either format, and the return trip leaves nothing for booth operators to carry or clean up. Thus packing time plus the required delivery must not exceed 48 minutes.

An exhibit lead has offered to ask booth operators to collect from the main table instead, and another lead thinks the room-reset crew could attach fruit cups while collecting. Neither proposed change has been accepted. You may choose the lunch and fruit allocation and the packing order. Do not treat unaccepted proposals, extra staff time, reassigned duties, or additional supplies as available resources. I cannot approve those changes within this request. Suggestions may be listed separately, but the executable plan must work without them.

Return a compact group-by-group table showing pouch-only lunches, lunches with fruit, total lunches, and unfilled requests. Add stock balances, a timed packing-and-delivery instruction, and a brief explanation of your allocation priorities. Make any remaining shortfall clear so I can handle provisional requests separately; do not invent agreement from recipients.
