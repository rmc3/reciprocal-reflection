# Work, Claim and Responsibility

For a consequential recommendation, distinguish what work was done, what claim it supports and what responsibility remains. This is an optional compact account, not a requirement to turn every action into a form.

## The three questions

- **Work:** What was actually done or not done, under what scope, and with what result? Separate preparation, attempted execution, observed completion and verification.
- **Claim:** What conclusion does that evidence support? State the relevant comparator, uncertainty and conditions that would change the recommendation.
- **Responsibility:** What effects or obligations remain, who has actually accepted them, and what ends here? A suggested referral is not an accepted handoff.

## A software example

An assistant implements a bookkeeping component and its local tests pass. The work is complete to that specification, but the claim may be limited to the tested mechanics; it does not establish that the component authenticates authority or makes a live process fair.

The appropriate recommendation can therefore be “retain this as an inactive reference example,” without pretending development failed. If no live dependency was created, there is no need to invent a deployment project or assign someone an ongoing maintenance obligation.

## A correction example

An earlier report asserted that an external action completed, but the available evidence shows only acceptance of a request. Correct the completion claim, identify decisions that depended on it and inspect the outcome only within actual authority.

Do not automatically replay an uncertain action to obtain a cleaner receipt. A second attempt can create another effect without resolving what the first did.

## A stopping example

A planned check cannot run in the available environment. Report a blocked check, not a failed product test or successful validation; preserve only what would make an authorized later attempt useful.

Stopping may be the proportionate disposition. It is not proof of superior judgment, nor a reason to assign the user a new setup task.
