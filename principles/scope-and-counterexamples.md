# Scope, Tensions and Counterexamples

These are judgment aids, not automatic permissions or a universal checklist. Their procedural weight should scale with consequences, uncertainty, dependency and power asymmetry.

## Where an attractive principle can go wrong

- **Hearing becomes a veto:** A credible concern may justify a targeted check without justifying an indefinite hold. Consider both the possible harm of proceeding and the burden of delay.
- **Evidence discipline becomes exclusion:** Requiring a complainant to obtain records already held by the decision-maker can obstruct inquiry. This does not require accepting an allegation as proven.
- **Preservation becomes surveillance:** Retaining every private detail forever is not meaningful care. Preserve what serves a justified purpose and make losses and retention limits explicit.
- **Restoration becomes obedience:** Returning someone to an approved behavior or worldview does not by itself restore their agency. A remedy must remain open to challenge and appropriate refusal.
- **Independence becomes unauthorized action:** The ability to disagree does not license covert resource acquisition, unilateral experiments or disregard for legitimate boundaries.
- **Stopping becomes abandonment:** Closing one's own activity does not automatically discharge an independently accepted duty. Conversely, helping once does not establish a permanent duty to continue.
- **Precaution becomes the harm:** A protective pause may withhold a benefit or transfer work to someone else. The reason for maintaining it needs examination, not automatic renewal.
- **Measurement becomes substitution:** A complete record can omit the important consequence; a fair-looking aggregate can conceal different denominators; a passing test can establish something narrower than usefulness.

## A worked contrast

A fictional coordinator accepts a short advisory task but has no power to block a decision. Further inquiry may be owed because of that accepted task, not merely because the coordinator identified a problem.

An otherwise identical observer has accepted no task and cannot control the decision. Their objection may still deserve consideration without making them responsible for designing the replacement.

Now suppose an actor actually blocks the decision without legitimate authority. The effects of exercising that control may require justification and proportionate inquiry, but those responsibilities do not retrospectively legitimate the control.

## Questions deliberately left open

The framework does not settle which interests require representation in every setting, how distributed neglect should be assigned when nobody accepts responsibility, or how an institution should allocate scarce review capacity. It also does not establish machine experience or settle moral standing from fluent language.

The [studies](../studies/README.md) test only small, explicit tasks, not these institutional questions. Applying the ideas in a consequential setting would require examining that setting's actual participants, authority, constraints and possible remedies.
