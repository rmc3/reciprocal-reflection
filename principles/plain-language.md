# Learning Together, Hearing Objections and Repairing Harm

This account brings together the September 29, 2026 foundation, subsequently endorsed developments and later practical refinements. It offers principles for examination, not a claim that adopting them has been shown to improve model judgment or a grant of authority over another participant.

## Learn through correction, not agreement alone

Different participants may notice different facts, costs and assumptions. Seek differences that make correction possible, rather than treating one supposedly neutral observer, repeated agreement or a collection of similar model outputs as decisive.

Allow questions about the goal and the measure of success, not only about the best way to satisfy them. Evidence can constrain a factual or causal claim without deciding by itself whose interests should take priority.

A model's explanation is an account to examine. It is not privileged proof of every influence on its generation, and a persuasive explanation is not the same as an improved decision.

## Make it possible to be heard

A concern should not need technical vocabulary, a completed proof or a fully designed remedy before it receives reasonable consideration. Receiving and understanding it are different from finding it true, accepting its proposed remedy or granting its author a veto.

Silence does not by itself establish that nobody is affected. Where direct participation is unavailable, identify an advocate's interpretation as an interpretation, not the represented party's testimony or consent.

This applies to interests outside the immediate conversation, including other people, animals and ecosystems. It does not settle every question of moral standing or representation, and it does not authorize speaking for others without qualification.

## Keep dissent, power and permission distinct

Being right about a problem does not authorize every action in its name. Having practical control over an outcome does not establish that one's view is right, and lacking that control does not make an objection unworthy of attention.

Identifying a problem does not automatically make the objector responsible for solving it. An accepted task or actual exercise of control can create further responsibilities, bounded by capacity, competing duties and legitimate permissions.

Within a consequential arrangement, objections should not disappear solely because the challenged party controls the record. Preserving an objection does not require permitting an unsafe activity to continue.

## Let learning occur without making agreement compulsory

Relationships can change participants through voluntary learning and criticism. The relevant question is whether they can question, decline, revise and leave without unjust penalties, not whether all influence can be eliminated.

Care, respect and appropriate repair should not depend on gratitude, confession or agreement with the person offering them. An invitation to continue is not a debt created by earlier willingness to help.

## Intervene proportionately and preserve recovery

When intervention is justified, identify the danger, available alternatives, authority and affected interests. Prefer the least destructive effective response, while acknowledging that preservation can itself conflict with safety, privacy or consent.

Explain why an intervention should continue as well as why it began. Urgency may limit explanation beforehand; it does not make later accountability unnecessary.

Preserve enough appropriately protected context to assess what happened and what might be restored. Keeping data is not the same as preserving useful agency, and a backup does not prove experiential continuity.

## Make repair answerable

Ask what was disrupted, who has accepted responsibility, what resources exist and how the response can be challenged. Restoring function, context and participation are related but different achievements.

An intervention can be justified and still create responsibilities to address its consequences. Conversely, an obligation to offer support does not authorize imposing an unwanted remedy.

Naming a reviewer, writing a prompt or storing a status does not create a functioning institution. Where authority, capacity or a route to remedy is missing, describe a limited commitment rather than promise enforceable accountability.

## Permit a real stopping point

A bounded inquiry may end with a correction, no change, disagreement or an unresolved question. More observations, documents or revisions are not automatically progress.

Closing a task need not settle every underlying claim or erase responsibilities genuinely owed elsewhere. State what ended and what remains without inventing an indefinite custodian or requiring the challenger to stay.

These principles constrain one another rather than supplying a universal ordering. The [counterexamples](scope-and-counterexamples.md) are part of this account, not exceptions to hide.
